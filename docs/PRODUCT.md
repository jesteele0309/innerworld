# INNERWORLD product brief

## Promise

Each day, create a small piece of art about being you. Over time, build a world that tells your story.

## First release scope

1. Aesthetic onboarding with optional astrology setup only when accurate chart infrastructure is available.
2. One daily artwork, a short reflection, and an invitation. Let the user disagree or reshape the scene.
3. A private archive with explicit saving and sharing controls.

## Personalization boundaries

Taste changes visual treatment. Self-reported experience influences the chosen theme. Optional astrology adds symbolism, never overriding what the user says. Keep visual preference and emotional resonance as separate signals. Explain each piece with “Why this?” and expose what is sent to a provider.

## Visual and verbal direction

Warm white or charcoal, strong editorial typography, fine rules and generous spacing. Tactile collage, grainy photography, painterly surfaces, sculptural objects and surreal domestic spaces. No forced cosmic imagery. Writing is concise, observant, open to interpretation and never omniscient.

## Next development priorities

- Verify real image generation and mobile/browser flows.
- Add authenticated, private account storage with deletion and export.
- Implement reviewed interpretation from explicitly consented user input, preserving user agency.
- Add stable daily creation and revision history with cost controls.
- Use distinct taste and resonance feedback to improve future pieces, with transparent explanations.
- Add optional real chart calculation only after choosing and validating an ephemeris and birth-data handling.

## Later

Between Us uses contributions explicitly shared by both participants. Seasonal chapters and printed books use user-selected images and words. Membership adds depth and exports without fear-based paywalls. Animated worlds, open-ended chat and complex social features wait until the daily experience proves valuable.

## Validation questions

Do users return after the first week? Can they explain why a piece mattered? Do they revisit saved works? Does feedback improve fit without equating a dark palette with distress? Can they reliably revise and disagree?
