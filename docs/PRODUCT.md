# INNERWORLD product brief

## Promise

Each day, make one small piece of art about being you. Over time, it adds up
to a world that tells your story.

## The loop

**Notice → Reflect → Make → Keep**, once a day, in about ten minutes.

| Step | What happens | What it costs the person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notice | A question and a pigment arrive. Three ways in: Read, Drift, Wander. | Two minutes, or none |
| Reflect | One prompt, one private answer, up to two feeling words. | A sentence |
| Make | Those words choose the palette. The person chooses the mark. | One tap |
| Keep | The portrait joins a chapter they name. | One tap |

Each step can be skipped. A day with nothing kept is drawn as a quiet day.

## The three design commitments

**1. The question is the content.** Topics are phrased as questions — *What did
a colour used to cost?* — because a question invites an answer and a noun does
not. Everyone gets the same question on the same day; nothing is inferred about
anyone from anything.

**2. Taste and resonance stay separate, and both are used.** Mark style is
taste: it changes how paint is laid down. Feeling words are resonance: they
choose which pigments appear. The Make screen states the derivation in plain
words — *"Indigo from today's question. Lead white and burnt umber from the
words you picked: tender and heavy."* Asking for feedback and then discarding
it is the one thing this product cannot afford.

**3. Nothing counts a run.** No streaks, no reminders, no notifications, no
score. Chapters show quiet days as quiet days, and today as still open.

## Personalisation boundaries

Taste changes visual treatment. Self-reported feeling changes the palette.
Neither is treated as a diagnosis, and the app never tells anyone what their
colours mean. Every piece explains itself under "Why this?", including the
exact prompt when a model was involved. A dark palette is never read as
distress.

## What is made by a person

Every question, essay, drift sequence and walk is written by hand. The painting
is the only generated artefact, and the screen where it is made says so. By
default it is drawn on the device; a model is opt-in and receives the question,
the palette and the mark style only.

## Next development priorities

1. **Choose real artwork.** The Read plates are placeholders. Pick public-domain
   images, verify each accession record, and ship the credits. A wrong credit
   would cost more than a visible blank.
2. **Narrated audio for Drift.** It is written to be read slowly; a voice would
   make it what it claims to be. Needs a recorded reader, not a synthesised one,
   to keep the human-made promise.
3. **Backup restore.** Export exists; import does not, so a backup is currently
   a dead file. Validate piece ids on the way in — untrusted JSON setting an id
   is the one place this app could grow an injection.
4. **More days.** Eight questions cycle. Thirty would make a chapter feel
   unrepeated; the content model takes new days with no code change.
5. **Chapter turning.** Chapters currently begin once and run forever. They
   should close on a cadence the person chooses, and the closing should be the
   moment a book becomes orderable.
6. **Accounts and server-side storage.** The gate on sync, shared chapters and
   printed books. It is also where the current privacy guarantees have to be
   rewritten rather than extended, so it waits until the daily loop has proved
   itself.

## Later

Shared chapters between two people who both opt in. Printed books from pieces
the person selects. Membership that adds depth rather than removing fear.
Nothing social until the solitary loop is worth returning to.

## Validation questions

Do people come back after the first week? Can they say why a particular piece
mattered? Do they revisit kept portraits? Does the palette derivation feel
true, or arbitrary? Can they disagree with a piece and reshape it? Does the
absence of streaks read as freedom or as drift?
